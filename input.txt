2a. Identifying the Problem Parameters
TRIZ (Theory of Inventive Problem Solving) is a systematic methodology for innovation developed by Genrich Altshuller. It provides structured tools to identify and resolve technical contradictions in product development. The TRIZ Prism approach (Ilevbare, Probers, & Phaal, 2013) guides this process in four steps. This assignment applies the TRIZ Prism to two product development problems of the Gentoo rover.
Problem 1: Speed vs. Energy Consumption 
Our initial product development problem relates to Gentoo’s driving behavior, a fully autonomous, solar-powered rover designed for a 1,000 km Antarctic traverse.The rover must optimize long-distance efficiency using only a 6m2 solar panel array. Gentoo has a mean operating speed of 3km/h. To achieve this optimization, this low driving speed is maintained without any acceleration causing kinetic losses (Sihag, 2026). However, this is a contradiction as it makes the rover very unresponsive to short-range movements, making it difficult to avoid obstacles and navigate through complicated terrain. If more frequent accelerations or faster speeds were allowed, then the short-range operational effectiveness would be improved, but this would increase the energy consumption, reducing the overall efficiency and energy budget (Gadd, 2011).
Problem 2: Turning Radius vs. Weight & Reliability 
Our second product development problem relates the rover’s turning radius and its direct effect on maneuverability. At the moment, Gentoo's large turning radius limits its capacity to maneuver efficiently in restricted spaces with lots of obstacles. The turning radius needs to be considerably decreased in order to provide operating flexibility. However, additional hardware would be required while implementing design solutions to succeed a smaller turning radius (ex: differential steering or other advanced steering mechanisms) (Sihag, 2026). This increases weight, energy consumption, and mechanical complexity, creating new failure points. System reliability is a crucial concern because of the severe Antarctic environment, where human intervention and maintenance are totally impossible (Gadd, 2011). Thus, to retain autonomous mission viability, a crucial trade-off must be made: the turning radius must be short enough to enable quick navigation, but the physical design must continue to be lightweight, energy-efficient, and extremely dependable. 







b. Categorization of Problem Parameters and Identification of Contradictions
The first product development issue is the tension between Gentoo's energy efficiency for long-distance travel and its responsiveness for short-distance travel. The average speed of the rover is about 3 km/h so that it doesn't exceed its energy budget. This approach will reduce acceleration and stopping losses thus maximizing use of the solar energy available. However, this decreases the rover's turn to turn when facing obstacles, and complex terrain. In the TRIZ paradigm the desired improvement can be classified under Parameter 9: Speed which is the velocity of an object or process. If the rover could accelerate more often or faster, the effectiveness of rover operations for short range missions would be enhanced. This improvement would directly raise the value of Parameter 19: Use of Energy by a Moving Object, in the TRIZ framework. The higher the energy consumption, the lower the mission efficiency will be in Gentoo, which is solar powered only, and may jeopardize mission completion. So there is a technical contradiction: to increase the response time and navigation performance of the rover, the speed needs to be increased, but if the rover requires a longer mission range, then the speed needs to be decreased. An operation being efficient will lead it to be slower, meaning that there is a trade off between speed and energy efficiency. 
Gentoo's turning radius and maneuverability is the second product development problem. The rover's large turning radius makes it less efficient on navigating the terrain. A smaller turning radius would be of great benefit to maneuverability and enable the rover to adjust to environmental conditions. From the TRIZ engineering parameters, the desired improvement falls under the category of Parameter 35: Adaptability or Versatility. However, to get a smaller turning radius, more steering mechanisms (differential steering, or more complex steering architectures) have to be added. These add weight and energy demands to the rover. The negative aspects can therefore be classified as Parameter 1: Weight of Moving Object, and Parameter 19: Use of Energy by Moving Object, both of which are important parameters for the operation of the rover on a solar powered basis in Antarctica. A second technical contradiction exists: the rover needs to be lightweight and energy efficient to ensure mission viability but also needs to have a smaller turning radius to increase maneuverability and adaptability. This inverse relationship must be overcome with TRIZ inventive principles.

2.c. Identification of Inventive Principles for Contradiction Resolution 

The contradiction matrix was used to identify inventive principles that would resolve the identified technical contradictions with the least negative effect on other engineering parameters. 

For the first contradiction, to improve Parameter 9 (Speed) without deteriorating Parameter 19 (Use of Energy by Moving Object), the matrix suggests using Inventive Principles 8 (Anti-Weight), 15 (Dynamics), 35 (Parameter Changes), and 38 (Accelerated Oxidation). Among these, Principles 15 and 35 are the most applicable to the Gentoo rover. Principle 15 (Dynamics) states that the characteristics of a system should be allowed to change according to operating conditions rather than being kept constant. Principle 35 (Parameter Changes) suggests changing operational parameters such as speed, acceleration, or control settings to improve performance while limiting negative side effects. These principles address the contradiction between responsiveness and energy efficiency. 

For the second problem of product development, two contradictions were taken into account. Improving Parameter 35 (Adaptability or Versatility) without worsening Parameter 1 (Weight of Moving Object) leads to Principles 1 (Segmentation), 6 (Universality), 15 (Dynamics), and 8 (Anti-Weight). Improving Parameter 35 without worsening Parameter 19 (Use of Energy by Moving Object) results in Principles 19 (Periodic Action), 35 (Parameter Changes), 29 (Pneumatics and Hydraulics), and 13 (The Other Way Round).

Among these principles, Segmentation, Universality, Dynamics, and Periodic Action were considered the most relevant to the Gentoo rover. Segmentation encourages dividing functions into smaller modules, Universality supports multifunctional components, Dynamics enables adaptation to changing conditions, and Periodic Action recommends activating functions only when needed. These inventive principles together provide promising ways for resolving the identified contradictions while maintaining the operational constraints and mission objectives of the rover.


2.d Generate Solutions 

To solve the contradiction of response speed vs. energy consumption, Principle 15 (Dynamics) was chosen for the first product development problem. Gentoo could make use of an adaptive driving strategy rather than driving at a fixed speed throughout the mission. In open areas, the rover would stay in its energy-saving mode and would move at a steady pace when traveling over greater distances. If the onboard sensors sense obstacles, crevasses, steep slopes, or other challenging terrain features, however, the control system would temporarily accelerate the speed, acceleration and steering response. Once the obstacle has been passed, the rover would automatically return to its normal energy-saving mode.

This solution enhances obstacle avoidance, but does not adversely affect the overall mission efficiency. High-performance mode uses more energy but activates only briefly, minimizing   
 total impact. . Moreover, the concept is based primarily on the modification of software and control systems, thus limiting weight and hardware complexity increases.

Principle 1 (Segmentation) was chosen for the second product development problem, to solve the contradiction between maneuverability and the increased weight of advanced steering mechanisms. Gentoo could take advantage of independent control of its left and right wheel systems, rather than installing a bigger steering assembly. In normal mode of operation, all wheels would work in concert to drive in an energy efficient manner. The wheel systems might be operated independently if the rover has to negotiate through small gaps or areas with obstacles or in cases of fine steering. The rover would slow and then turn the wheels the other way while the opposite side of the vehicle was still moving forward, thereby increasing the rate at which the other side of the vehicle turns and minimizing the turning radius.

This solution will provide for a more maneuverable system with limited extra hardware. It is largely built on the wheel drive system already developed for the rover, which limits the increase in mass and energy consumption. The simple mechanism preserves reliability, critical for an autonomous rover where repairs are impossible. As a result, the Gentoo will have a better ability to navigate and will retain mission capability, energy efficiency, and operation reliability.




2.e Trade-off Analysis Using Conjoint Analysis 

To evaluate the proposed solutions, a conjoint analysis will assess trade-offs among the rover's core attributes: energy consumption, operational velocity, turning radius, system weight, and maneuverability. Each attribute is assigned discrete levels (e.g., low/medium/high energy usage; compact/standard turning radius). Based on the adaptive driving and steering concepts, distinct rover configurations will be generated and ranked by engineering experts regarding their feasibility for Antarctic missions. The resulting preference data will be analyzed to estimate the part-worth utilities of each attribute level and determine their relative importance. This quantitative approach identifies the optimal configuration that balances energy efficiency and maneuverability while ensuring Gentoo remains lightweight, autonomous, and capable of long-distance polar operations.
