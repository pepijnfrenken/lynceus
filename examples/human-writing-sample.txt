A Clean Human-Written Sample

The rain started just after lunch, soft at first and then steady enough to darken the pavement. I had planned to spend the afternoon running errands, but the weather gave me an excuse to stay inside and finish the small things I had been avoiding.

I made tea, opened the kitchen window a little, and sorted through the stack of papers on the table. Most of it was ordinary clutter: receipts, appointment cards, a library notice, and a grocery list from two weeks ago. Still, clearing it away made the room feel calmer.

By four o'clock the sky had turned pale again. The rainwater moved along the curb in narrow streams, carrying leaves toward the drain at the corner. A neighbor walked past with a dog that clearly wanted to stop at every puddle. She laughed, tugged the leash gently, and kept going.

Nothing remarkable happened that day, which may be why I remember it. It had the quiet shape of an ordinary afternoon: a few chores completed, a little weather watched from indoors, and the simple relief of having nowhere urgent to be.
